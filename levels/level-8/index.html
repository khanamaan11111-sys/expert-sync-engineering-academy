Billing & Cost Control

Learn how quantities, valuations, changes, payments, cost reports and forecasts connect in a controlled QS workflow.

LESSON 01

Construction Billing Fundamentals

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Understand how measured work becomes a payment/valuation record.

Construction billing and valuation depend on the contract, role and project procedure. A QS must distinguish measured work, approved rates, supporting records, previous amounts and current-period values. Do not assume every project uses the same certificate or billing format.

Current-period value = cumulative verified value − previously recognized value, where the project method uses cumulative valuation.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Create a training billing register with Item, BOQ Qty, Rate, Previous Qty, Current Qty, Cumulative Qty and Amount.

LESSON 02

Measurement & Supporting Records

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Connect billed quantities to verifiable site/project evidence.

Quantities claimed or valued should be supported by the records required by the project: approved drawings, measurement sheets, site records, inspection/approval records or other contractual evidence. The exact evidence varies by contract and organization.

Traceability chain: BOQ Item → Location → Measurement → Supporting Record → Valuation.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Take five BOQ items and build a supporting-document checklist for each.

LESSON 03

Interim / Running Valuation Workflow

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Understand periodic valuation without treating one format as universal.

Many projects value work periodically. The workflow can include measured completed work, applicable approved rates, prior valuations, defined adjustments and review. Terminology such as interim payment, running bill or RA bill varies by market/project.

Cumulative quantity should reconcile with Previous + Current quantity when the project's workbook uses that structure.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Create a 10-item training monthly valuation using previous and current quantities.

LESSON 04

Variations & Change Control

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Track instructed/approved changes separately from the original scope.

Variations or change orders can affect quantities, scope and pricing. Maintain a register showing reference, description, status, quantity/cost impact and supporting approval according to contract procedure. Never treat an unapproved change as approved merely because it was measured.

Original BOQ and change items should remain traceable rather than silently merging scope.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Create a five-entry training variation register with status fields.

LESSON 05

Progress & Earned Value Basics

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Compare physical/valued progress with an agreed baseline carefully.

Cost-control reporting may compare planned/budget values with actual or earned values, but formal earned-value terminology and calculations require a defined baseline and methodology. Keep basic QS progress reporting separate from unsupported performance claims.

Basic training variance = Actual/Recorded Cost − Budget/Target Cost; interpret sign according to the reporting convention used.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Build a simple planned-vs-recorded cost table for five work sections.

LESSON 06

Committed, Actual & Forecast Cost

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Understand the difference between different cost views.

Cost control is stronger when commitments, recorded/actual costs and forecast-to-complete are not mixed together. Definitions depend on company systems. Document cut-off date and source for every report.

Forecast Final Cost = Cost to Date + Forecast to Complete, when that project reporting model applies.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Create a section-level cost report with Budget, Commitment, Cost to Date, Forecast to Complete and Forecast Final Cost.

LESSON 07

Cash Flow & Payment Tracking

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Track timing of valuations and payments without confusing value with cash.

Valuation, certification/invoice status and cash receipt/payment can occur at different times. A payment tracker should record dates, amounts, status and references. Cash-flow forecasts are estimates, not guarantees.

Outstanding = recognized/certified amount − paid amount, subject to the project's accounting definitions.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Create a six-period training payment tracker and identify outstanding balances.

LESSON 08

Cost Variance & Forecasting

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Identify cost movements and explain their drivers.

Variance analysis should show what changed and why: quantity movement, rate movement, approved scope change, productivity/resource effects or other documented causes. Forecasts should state assumptions and uncertainty.

Variance report: Work Section | Baseline/Target | Current Forecast | Variance | Driver | Action/Note.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Prepare a five-section variance report and explain the two largest movements.

LESSON 09

Billing & Cost-Control QA

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Apply checks before issuing or relying on a report.

Check quantities, units, rates, previous/cumulative logic, formulas, duplicates, change status, supporting references, cut-off dates and summary reconciliation. Formal approval must follow project authority; a spreadsheet check is not an approval.

Detailed totals → section totals → summary should reconcile, with exceptions investigated.
📊 PRACTICAL QS TASK

Apply a 12-point QA checklist to your training valuation and cost report.

LESSON 10

Level 8 Billing & Cost Control Project

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Build a connected monthly QS commercial-control package.

Combine your Level 6 BOQ and Level 7 rate work with a monthly valuation, change register, cost report, payment tracker, variance analysis and QA checklist. Use clearly labelled training assumptions and preserve traceability.

Deliverable package: valuation + supporting register + variation register + cost summary + payment tracker + variance note + QA checklist.

Complete one simulated monthly commercial-control cycle and write a one-page management summary.
