POWER ELECTRONICS · LEVEL 08 / 10

Renewable Energy Applications

Study solar photovoltaic (PV) generation, power conversion, maximum power point tracking (MPPT) and battery-system principles.

Objectives

Understand the PV energy chain, calculate power and efficiency, explain MPPT and identify essential system protections.

1. Solar conversion chain

PV modules produce variable DC electricity. A DC–DC converter can adapt the operating point or bus voltage. An inverter can provide controlled AC to an appropriate load or compliant grid interface. A battery may connect through a suitable bidirectional converter and battery-management system (BMS).

2. PV power and MPPT

P = V × I. At 30 V and 8 A, PV output is 240 W. The maximum power point (MPP) is the operating point with the highest available V × I under prevailing conditions. Irradiance, temperature and shading affect the PV curve.

MPPT adjusts the operating point to seek available power; it does not create energy. Perturb-and-Observe compares power before and after small operating-point changes. Incremental Conductance uses the relationship dP/dV = I + V(dI/dV), equal to zero at a smooth local maximum. Both methods have practical limitations.

3. Converter and energy examples

A 250 W input at 92% converter efficiency gives approximately 230 W output. A steady 200 W delivered for three hours equals 600 Wh = 0.6 kWh; actual solar output varies.

4. Guided simulation exercise

Using a documented PV model in your own simulator, start at illustrative irradiance 1000 W/m² and cell temperature 25°C. Plot I–V and P–V curves; identify the simulated MPP. Model a low-voltage boost stage and compare fixed operating-point control against an MPPT approach during an irradiance change. Record assumptions, parameters, transients and measured power. These conditions do not imply a specific wattage without a module specification.

5. Protection and safety

Battery charging must respect chemistry, voltage, current and thermal limits and use a suitable BMS. Grid-connected inverters require appropriate synchronization, protection and compliance. PV strings can remain energized in sunlight and batteries can deliver high fault current. This educational lesson is not an installation procedure; use simulation first and qualified supervision for high-energy hardware.

6. Practice

1. Find PV power at 28 V and 9 A. 2. Find output power for 300 W input at 90% efficiency. 3. Explain why an MPPT controller responds to changing irradiance.

Suggested answers

1. 252 W. 2. 270 W. 3. The PV power curve and its maximum operating point can change.
