Solar PV & Energy Calculator

Practice photovoltaic power, simple energy estimates and conversion-chain losses using your own values. This is an educational calculator, not a PV installation, battery-sizing or financial-yield design tool.

1. PV operating power

P = V × I
This is power at the specified operating point, not necessarily the module's maximum power point.

2. Conversion chain

Output = Input × η₁ × η₂
Assumes constant stage efficiencies at the same operating point and excludes other system losses.

3. Simple energy estimate

Energy (Wh) = average power × hours
Use actual average power for the selected duration; sunshine and PV output are variable.

Worked exercise

A PV module operates at 32 V and 7.5 A. Its power is 240 W. If a DC–DC converter operates at 90% and a subsequent inverter at 95%, the estimated delivered power is 240 × 0.90 × 0.95 = 205.2 W. If that power is held constant for 2 hours, energy is 410.4 Wh. Enter these values above to reproduce the results.

Extra practice questions and answers
  1. PV: 28 V × 9 A = 252 W.
  2. 300 W through stages at 90% and 95% = 256.5 W.
  3. 150 W average for 4 hours = 600 Wh = 0.6 kWh.

How this connects to MPPT

These calculations do not determine the maximum power point. MPPT requires observing or modeling the PV I–V/P–V behavior and adjusting the operating point. Irradiance, temperature and shading can change the available power. For a guided simulation exercise, study Level 8 — Renewable Energy Applications.

Safety: PV modules may remain energized in sunlight. Battery systems and grid connections require suitable protection, compliant equipment and qualified supervision. Do not use this simplified tool alone to specify or connect hardware.
