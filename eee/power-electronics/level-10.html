POWER ELECTRONICS · LEVEL 10 / 10 · FINAL LESSON

Portfolio & Interview Readiness

Turn your converter calculations, simulation exercises and design project into a credible engineering portfolio. Practice explaining technical choices clearly without claiming hardware validation or certifications you have not earned. Estimated study time: 60–90 minutes.

Learning outcomes

  • Prepare an organized, evidence-based engineering project portfolio.
  • Present a converter design with assumptions, equations and measured simulation results.
  • Answer foundational power-electronics interview questions.
  • Describe limitations, safety considerations and next steps professionally.

1. Build your portfolio

Create a PDF or webpage containing a short profile, relevant skills and two or three carefully documented projects. For each project include:

  1. Problem statement and measurable specifications.
  2. Selected topology and justification.
  3. Assumptions, equations and component calculations.
  4. Simulation environment, model parameters and representative waveforms.
  5. Comparison of predicted and simulated values, discrepancies and limitations.
  6. Your own contributions, references and proposed improvements.

Use descriptive file names and legible diagrams. Label all axes, values and units. Do not present simulated results as experimental hardware measurements.

2. Present the Level 9 buck converter project

Example summary: “I modeled an idealized 24 V-to-12 V, 2 A buck converter at 100 kHz. Under ideal CCM assumptions, the duty cycle was 0.5. I estimated a 150 μH inductor for about 0.4 A peak-to-peak ripple, then compared the predicted output and ripple with simulated steady-state waveforms. The project is a simulation study, not a certified hardware design.”

Be prepared to explain why the capacitor estimate ignores ESR, why real efficiency differs from an ideal model, and how load variation or a feedback loop would change the results.

3. Technical interview revision

What is the difference between a rectifier and an inverter?

A rectifier converts AC to DC; an inverter converts DC to controlled AC. Real implementations require appropriate switching, filtering and protection.

What is the ideal CCM buck relationship?

Vout ≈ D × Vin, where D is duty cycle. This assumes ideal steady-state CCM operation.

How does an ideal boost converter work?

Its ideal CCM relationship is Vout ≈ Vin/(1 − D) for 0 ≤ D < 1. Real losses and operating constraints prevent unlimited voltage gain.

What do MOSFETs and IGBTs do?

They are controlled semiconductor switches selected according to voltage, current, switching-frequency, thermal and application requirements.

Why use PWM in an inverter?

PWM controls the switching pattern to shape the desired fundamental output and move significant switching harmonics to frequencies suitable for filtering or the application.

Why is feedback used?

A measured output is compared with a reference so a controller can adjust the command and improve regulation and disturbance response, provided the loop is designed for stability.

What is MPPT?

Maximum power point tracking adjusts the operating point of a PV source to seek its highest available power under prevailing conditions.

How is converter efficiency calculated?

Efficiency = output power / input power × 100%, using consistently measured or averaged powers at the same operating condition.

4. Interview response structure

Use a concise technical sequence: Objective → Design choice → Calculation → Simulation evidence → Result → Limitation. If you do not know an answer, identify what you would verify rather than inventing a specification or test result.

For behavioral questions, explain your individual contribution, an obstacle, the action you took and the measurable or observable outcome. Avoid claiming employment, laboratory access, licences or certificates solely from completing these lessons.

5. Final readiness checklist

  • Explain the difference between buck, boost, rectifier and inverter topologies.
  • Show your Level 9 calculations and labeled simulation plots.
  • Discuss device ratings, switching losses, ripple, control and safety.
  • State the limitations of ideal models and proposed next validation steps.
  • Prepare a two-minute project explanation and a one-page resume tailored to the role.

6. Practice assignment

Prepare a five-slide technical presentation: 1) objective/specification, 2) topology and assumptions, 3) design calculations, 4) simulation waveforms and comparison, 5) conclusions and limitations. Rehearse a two-minute explanation. Include references and distinguish predicted, simulated and experimentally measured values.

Safety: These educational projects do not authorize unsupervised mains-connected or high-energy hardware work. Physical prototypes require qualified supervision and proper protections.
