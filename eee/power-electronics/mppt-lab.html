Solar MPPT Simulation Lab

Explore a simplified photovoltaic power–voltage curve and step through a basic perturb-and-observe (P&O) tracking algorithm. This deliberately uses a synthetic teaching model, not measured PV module characteristics or a real-time physical converter simulation.

1. Configure the example PV curve

Teaching curve: P(V) = Ppeak × max(0, 1 − ((V − Vmpp)/Vmpp)²), evaluated from 0 to 2 × Vmpp. It is symmetric by construction and is not an actual PV I–V model. For V > 0, displayed current is P/V; current at zero voltage is intentionally not calculated.

Press Reset to initialize the tracking exercise.

2. Tracking history

The tracker perturbs its voltage reference, compares power with the previous measurement and reverses direction when power decreases. A fixed step may cause oscillation around the modeled maximum.

3. Practice and limitations

  1. Set Vmpp to 30 V and Ppeak to 240 W. What is the model's maximum power?
  2. Try 2 V steps, then reset and try 0.5 V steps. Compare convergence and oscillation.
  3. Change the modeled peak while tracking. Why must the algorithm respond to a changing power curve?
Suggested answers

1) 240 W at 30 V in this teaching model. 2) Smaller steps generally offer finer tracking but may take more iterations. 3) A changed power curve changes the operating point that yields maximum modeled power.

Scope: This is a static browser exercise. It omits irradiance and temperature physics, partial shading, local maxima, switching dynamics, converter constraints, measurement noise and protection. Never use it to control physical PV or battery hardware.
