Practical Design Project

Design and evaluate an idealized 24 V to 12 V buck converter in simulation. This is a structured learning project, not a validated hardware design or construction guide. Estimated study time: 60–90 minutes.

Learning outcomes

Translate requirements into calculations, estimate component values, simulate a converter, assess ripple and losses, and produce a technical report that states assumptions and limitations.

1. Project specification

ParameterIllustrative target
Input voltage24 V DC (ideal source)
Output voltage12 V DC nominal
Output current2 A nominal
Output power24 W nominal
Switching frequency100 kHz
Target inductor rippleApproximately 20% of output current, peak-to-peak
Output ripple targetLess than 1% peak-to-peak in the idealized steady-state model

Assume continuous-conduction mode (CCM), ideal components and a resistive nominal load for the initial calculation. Document any later changes.

2. Preliminary calculations

Nominal load: R = V/I = 12/2 = 6 Ω. Ideal buck duty cycle: D = Vout/Vin = 12/24 = 0.5.

Inductor ripple target: 20% × 2 A = 0.4 A peak-to-peak.

Using the ideal CCM approximation ΔIL = (Vin − Vout) × D / (L × fs), rearrange to L = (Vin − Vout) × D / (ΔIL × fs).

L = (24 − 12) × 0.5 / (0.4 × 100000) = 150 μH.

For an ideal capacitor with triangular ripple current and ignoring ESR, approximate ΔVpp ≈ ΔIL / (8 × fs × C). To target 0.12 V peak-to-peak (1% of 12 V), the ideal lower estimate is C ≈ 0.4/(8 × 100000 × 0.12) = 4.17 μF. This is only a first-order model; real capacitor ESR, tolerances, transient response and ripple-current rating matter.

Estimated inductor current at nominal load varies around 2 A by ±0.2 A, giving approximately 1.8 A to 2.2 A in this idealized CCM case. This is not a final component-current rating.

3. Simulation workflow

  1. Create an idealized buck circuit in an appropriate simulator: DC source, switching device, freewheeling path, 150 μH inductor, capacitor, 6 Ω load, PWM source and measurements.
  2. Begin with a simulated 0.5 duty cycle and 100 kHz switching frequency. Choose an appropriate solver or time step to resolve switching behavior.
  3. Plot switch-node voltage, inductor current and output voltage. Allow startup to settle before measuring averages and ripple.
  4. Compare measured average output with 12 V and inductor ripple with approximately 0.4 A peak-to-peak.
  5. Vary the load in simulation and record how current, ripple and output change. If introducing nonideal models, include device losses and controller behavior explicitly.

Exact model blocks depend on your simulator and version. An ideal fixed-duty converter may not hold 12 V under all nonideal conditions; feedback is a separate design consideration.

4. Evaluation and documentation

Record your specification, schematic or block diagram, parameter table, assumptions, calculations, steady-state waveform captures and comparison of predicted versus simulated results. For nonideal models, calculate efficiency using consistently averaged input/output power over a steady-state interval: η = Pout/Pin × 100%.

Explain discrepancies, startup behavior, duty-cycle limits, possible component stress and what additional validation would be required before any physical implementation.

Suggested project report outline

Title → Objective → Specification → Topology selection → Assumptions → Calculations → Simulation model → Waveforms → Comparison table → Limitations → Conclusion → References.

  2. At 24 V input and 25% ideal buck duty cycle, what is the predicted output?
  3. For 30 W input and 27 W output, what is the efficiency?

6. Safety and limitations

This project is for simulation and supervised low-voltage education. Do not connect experimental converters to household mains, high-energy batteries or PV strings. Real hardware requires qualified review of device ratings, protection, layout, isolation, thermal behavior and measurement methods. Capacitors can retain charge after power-off.
